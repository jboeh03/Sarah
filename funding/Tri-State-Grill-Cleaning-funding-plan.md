## The honest reality check (read this first)

You want one of three things: a **small line of credit**, an **unsecured business card**,
or **fast short-term money**. Here's the truth about each for your exact situation:

1. **"EIN-only, no personal guarantee, no credit check" cards are mostly out of reach
   right now — and the ads promising them to bad-credit owners are usually scams.**
   The legit no-personal-guarantee cards (Ramp, Brex, Rho) decide based on your *business
   bank balance and revenue* — they typically want **$25,000–$100,000+ sitting in a
   business account** or six-figure annual revenue, and usually a registered company. They
   are not bad-credit products. If you see "guaranteed approval, EIN only, $50K, no credit
   check," treat it as a scam or an expensive credit-repair upsell. ([Nav][nav-ein],
   [Ramp][ramp-ein])

2. **Almost every normal small-business card and bank line of credit will pull your
   personal credit and require a personal guarantee.** Bad personal credit doesn't always
   mean auto-decline (some approve in the high-500s), but it's the single biggest gate.

3. **What actually works for a cash-flowing service business with weak personal credit is
   a different lane:** build a business credit file from scratch, use products judged on
   *revenue/deposits* instead of FICO, and use *character-based* community lenders. That's
   the plan below.

The good news: hood/grill cleaning is a **real, recurring-revenue service** restaurants pay
for on a schedule. Consistent bank deposits are exactly what the revenue-based lenders care
about — that's your leverage, not your credit score.

## Step 1 — Build business credit *without* your personal credit

These don't hinge on a good FICO and report your payments to the **business** bureaus
(D&B / Experian Business), which is how you eventually qualify for better terms.

- **Net-30 vendor accounts that report** — buy real supplies you already need (degreaser,
  bags, filters, PPE, scrapers) and pay the invoice in 30 days. Common starter accounts
  that often **don't require personal credit**: Uline, Quill, Grainger, Summa Office
  Supplies, Crown Office Supplies. ([Nav][nav-ein])
- **Store / fuel cards** for a service vehicle (Home Depot/Lowe's commercial, fleet fuel
  cards) — easier to get than a real credit card and they report.

Three reporting accounts paid on time for a few months builds a business score from zero.

## Step 2 — A card you can actually get now

- **Secured business credit card** — e.g., **Bank of America Business Advantage Unlimited
  Cash Secured**: you put down a deposit (min ~$1,000) and that becomes your limit. It
  reports to business bureaus and can **graduate** to unsecured over time. Approval doesn't
  ride on having great personal credit. ([BofA][bofa-secured])
- **Fallback to also rebuild your personal credit** (because it gates the cheaper money
  later): **OpenSky Secured Visa** — **no credit check, no bank account required**, you pick
  the limit with a $200–$3,000 deposit. It's a personal card, but rebuilding your personal
  score quietly widens every future option. ([Expensify][expensify-nocredit])
- **Capital One Spark Classic** is built for *fair* credit — possibly a stretch with "bad,"
  but worth a soft-check pre-qualification if your score is mid-600s.

## Step 3 — Short-term / immediate money (judged on revenue, not FICO)

This is the most realistic answer to "line of credit or short-term funding" for you —
**but only after you have a few months of deposits in the business account.**

**If you accept card payments** (Square, PayPal, or Stripe reader/invoices):
- **Square Loans / PayPal Working Capital / Stripe Capital** — offers are based on your
  **payment-processing volume**, often with **no traditional credit pull**, and you repay
  automatically as a percentage of daily sales. For a service business that runs card
  payments, this is often the easiest fast cash to qualify for. (Tip: start running jobs
  through one of these processors now to build the history that triggers an offer.)

**Line of credit with a low FICO bar:**
- **Fundbox** — line of credit, **~600 minimum personal score**, decision in minutes,
  funds in **1–2 business days**. Judged heavily on bank-account activity. ([Lendio][lendio-loc])

**⚠️ Be careful with Merchant Cash Advances / aggressive "revenue-based" offers.** They
quote a **factor rate** (e.g., 1.3 = pay back $1.30 per $1) instead of an APR, which can
work out to an **effective 40–100%+ APR**. Use them only for a short, clearly
profit-positive need (e.g., a job you're already contracted for), never to cover ongoing
shortfalls, and **never stack multiple advances**. ([Lendio bad-credit][lendio-badcredit],
[Bankrate][bankrate-badcredit])
